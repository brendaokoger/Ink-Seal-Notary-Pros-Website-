Corporate & Commercial Document Apostille

◆

Businesses operating internationally, foreign investors acquiring US entities, and legal professionals handling cross-border transactions frequently require apostille authentication for corporate and commercial documents. Ink & Seal Notary Pros provides careful document review and apostille coordination for business records and commercial agreements.

Who This Service Is For

  • Business owners with US entities expanding into foreign markets
  • Foreign investors and their representatives acquiring or partnering with US companies
  • Attorneys and paralegals managing international corporate matters
  • Companies executing international contracts that require authenticated supporting documents
  • Commercial lenders and financial institutions with cross-border documentation requirements

Common Corporate Documents for Apostille

  • Articles of incorporation and articles of organization
  • Certificates of good standing (from Secretary of State)
  • Corporate bylaws, operating agreements, and shareholder agreements
  • Powers of attorney for business purposes
  • Notarized affidavits and corporate resolutions
  • Commercial agreements and international contracts
  • Trade documents and certificates of origin

The Corporate Apostille Process

1
Document Review

We evaluate your corporate document and confirm apostille eligibility, identifying the correct processing authority and any preparatory steps required.

2
Notarization (if required)

Certain corporate documents — such as bylaws, operating agreements, and powers of attorney — must be notarized before the state will issue an apostille. We coordinate this step when needed.

3
Secretary of State Filing

The document is submitted to the appropriate state authority — typically the Secretary of State — for apostille certification.

4
Return

Your authenticated document is returned via tracked, insured shipping, with confirmation of completed processing.

A Note on Certificates of Good Standing

Certificates of good standing and similar state-issued corporate records are among the most straightforward corporate documents to apostille, as they are already issued and authenticated by the state. Other corporate documents — such as bylaws, operating agreements, and powers of attorney — may require notarization first. We confirm the correct approach for each document during the review step.
