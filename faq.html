Apostille

An apostille is an official government certification that authenticates a public document for use in a foreign country that belongs to the Hague Apostille Convention. It verifies the signature, seal, or stamp on the document — not its content — so it can be legally recognized abroad.

Any country that has joined the Hague Apostille Convention accepts apostilles — more than 120 member countries, including most of Europe, Latin America, and many countries in Asia and Africa. If your destination country is not a member, a different process called embassy or consulate legalization is required instead.

A Hague Convention country is a nation that has signed and ratified the Hague Apostille Convention of 1961, a treaty that standardizes how public documents are authenticated between member nations, replacing the need for multi-step embassy legalization.

It depends on where your document is going. If the destination country is a Hague Convention member, you need an apostille. If it is not a member, you need embassy or consulate legalization, a separate multi-step process. We confirm which process applies to your specific situation as part of your apostille review, before any processing begins.
