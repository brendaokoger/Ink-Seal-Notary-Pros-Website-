    Frequently Asked Questions

    Quick answers to the questions we hear most.

    An apostille is an official government certification that authenticates a public document for use in a foreign country that belongs to the Hague Apostille Convention. It verifies the signature, seal, or stamp on the document — not its content — so it can be legally recognized abroad.

    It depends on your document type and where it will be used. Documents such as birth certificates, marriage certificates, diplomas, and business records commonly require an apostille for international use.
